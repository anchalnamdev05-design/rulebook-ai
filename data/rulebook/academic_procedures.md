# University Rulebook — Academic Procedures

## 2.1 — Course Registration and Add-Drop

Students register for courses through the academic portal during the published registration period. A registration is complete only after the course appears in the student schedule and any required prerequisite check has passed. Students should inspect the schedule for clashes, duplicate registrations, and missing laboratory components. A student who discovers an error submits a correction request to the department office with the course code, section, and a description of the problem. The department may move a student between parallel sections when capacity, timetable, and academic prerequisites permit. A request to add a course after teaching has begun is reviewed by the programme chair and normally requires evidence that the student can complete missed work. Dropping a course does not erase an assessment already submitted and may affect credit progression, scholarship standing, or residence eligibility.

The add-drop period ends on the date listed in the academic calendar. After that date, withdrawal requires a documented reason and approval from the Dean of the Faculty. Students should not stop attending a course while waiting for a decision because an unapproved absence remains an absence. The registrar records the effective date of an approved withdrawal and informs the instructor, finance office, and student services office when the decision affects fees or records. A student who has a timetable issue should raise it promptly; informal permission from a classmate or teaching assistant does not amend the official registration. Registration records are the controlling record for examination entry and transcript production.

## 2.2 — Course Outlines and Assessment Notice

Each course outline states learning outcomes, required materials, contact hours, assessment components, weighting, submission channels, and important dates. The instructor discusses the outline during the first teaching week and provides a copy through the learning platform. An instructor may explain a procedure during class, but a change that affects marks or eligibility must be published in an updated notice. Students are responsible for checking the learning platform and institutional email, while departments are responsible for making notices accessible and retaining the published version. If two notices appear to differ, the student should preserve both versions and ask the programme office for a written clarification rather than relying on an informal message.

Assessment instructions must identify the permitted collaboration level and the method for submitting work. A technical failure should be reported to the platform help desk before the deadline where possible, with a screenshot, time, and file copy. A student should not repeatedly upload a corrupted file without contacting the instructor. The instructor records the submitted version and the time received. Reasonable accessibility adjustments approved through student services apply to the assessment arrangement stated in the approval and do not automatically change the underlying learning outcome. Students may ask for clarification of an ambiguous instruction before submission; a request after marks are released is handled through the academic review and appeal procedures.

## 2.3 — Laboratory and Practical Attendance

Laboratory sessions are scheduled teaching sessions and are included in the course attendance register. A student who misses a laboratory should contact the instructor and ask whether a make-up activity is available. A make-up does not automatically remove the absence from the register and may be offered only when equipment, safety, and learning outcomes allow it. Students must follow safety instructions, wear required protective equipment, and sign the practical attendance record using their own identity. Signing for another person is academic misconduct. An instructor may exclude a student from a practical activity when required safety training has not been completed, but the reason and any route to complete the training should be recorded.

Practical work is assessed using the criteria in the course outline. Students should label samples, maintain a dated laboratory notebook, and submit results through the specified channel. Group work may allow shared observations, but each student remains responsible for the accuracy of their own analysis and reflection. A result that appears inconsistent should be discussed with the instructor rather than altered to fit an expected conclusion. The laboratory coordinator may retain raw records for quality review. Students seeking medical or accessibility accommodation should contact the relevant office before a practical where possible so a safe alternative can be considered.

## 2.4 — Academic Advising and Progress Review

Every student is assigned an academic adviser or programme contact. Advising supports course planning, credit checks, progression questions, and referral to welfare or accessibility services. An advising meeting does not replace formal approval from the registrar, finance office, examination controller, or committee. The student should bring a current transcript, proposed schedule, and a list of unresolved registration issues. Advisers may explain likely consequences but do not promise a decision reserved for another officer. A written decision or portal record is required when a request changes a student record, exemption, deadline, or academic standing.

The Academic Progress Committee reviews published results and cases referred under the progression rules. A student may be asked to provide a statement about failed courses, approved leave, health circumstances, or a proposed completion plan. The committee considers the academic record and applicable policy; it does not substitute a private agreement for a published requirement. The decision states whether progression is allowed, conditional, deferred, or refused and identifies any action or review route. Students should meet a deadline in the decision even if they intend to appeal, unless the decision expressly suspends that requirement.

## 2.5 — Completion and Graduation Clearance

A candidate for graduation must complete the credit requirements of the programme, satisfy compulsory course requirements, return university property, and clear outstanding finance or residence matters. The registrar checks the academic record against the approved programme structure. A student who believes a completed course is missing from the audit should submit the transcript, result notice, and course approval evidence before the graduation clearance deadline. The check does not change a grade or waive a failed requirement. Those issues use the separate academic review process.

The graduation office publishes a clearance timetable and may require a final confirmation of name spelling, identity details, and contact information. A student who cannot attend a ceremony may request certificate collection or an approved representative procedure if one is published for that cycle. Ceremony attendance is not the same as academic completion. The official award date is the date recorded by the registrar after all requirements have been verified. Students should rely on the official award record rather than a provisional ceremony list.

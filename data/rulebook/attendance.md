# University Rulebook — Attendance

## 3.1 — Minimum Attendance Requirement

Students must maintain at least 75% attendance in each registered course to be eligible to appear for the final examination. Attendance is calculated from the first scheduled teaching session through the final instructional day. The register includes lectures, tutorials, and practical sessions identified in the approved course outline. A student should review attendance during the published correction window and report a missing or inaccurate entry to the course instructor with supporting evidence. The department reviews unresolved register questions before the examination timetable is published. Students below the threshold are normally not issued an examination hall ticket unless another express provision in the rulebook applies.

Attendance is not transferred between courses. A high attendance percentage in one subject cannot compensate for a shortfall in another. Students are responsible for signing or recording attendance through the method announced by the instructor and should report a technical problem on the day it occurs. Participation in an approved university activity may be recorded as authorised attendance when the activity coordinator sends confirmation to the department. Travel, personal errands, and ordinary timetable confusion do not automatically create authorised attendance. The instructor may correct an administrative recording error, but cannot privately waive the published eligibility requirement.

## 3.2 — Attendance Records and Review

At the beginning of a term, the department publishes the course meeting pattern and the way attendance will be recorded. Each instructor keeps a dated register or electronic record. Where a class is cancelled and later replaced, the replacement session is treated according to the notice issued for that session. A student who believes the record is wrong submits the date, course, and evidence through the department channel. The correction window exists so errors can be reviewed before examination eligibility is finalised; it is not a general late-excuse procedure.

The department communicates the calculated percentage before the final examination period where the timetable permits. A student may ask for a review of the arithmetic or an omitted record. The review decision identifies the records checked and the final percentage. If a student has a separately approved accommodation or exemption, the approval document must identify the course and period. A general email saying that a student was unwell is not by itself an approved exemption. Students should keep copies of requests, replies, and supporting documents.

## 3.3 — Approved Academic Activities

Representing the university at an approved academic, cultural, sporting, or service activity may be considered for attendance adjustment when the activity was authorised in advance and the coordinator confirms the dates. The adjustment is entered only for the affected course meetings and does not guarantee examination eligibility. The student must still meet any separate assessment, laboratory, or safety requirement. An activity that was not approved in advance is reviewed under the ordinary attendance process and may not qualify for an adjustment.

Departments apply the same recording process to all students. The coordinator provides the participant list, activity dates, and supervising officer. A student who believes a name was omitted asks the coordinator to correct the list rather than asking a classmate to alter the register. The department may ask for evidence of actual participation. Approved activities do not apply to private employment, tourism, family events, or personal travel unless a separate published rule specifically says otherwise.

## 3.4 — Attendance and Assessment Planning

Students should monitor attendance early enough to identify a risk before the examination period. An instructor or adviser may explain the current record and suggest lawful academic support, but advice does not change a percentage. A student who is repeatedly absent should contact the programme office and ask about support, leave, accessibility, or medical procedures as appropriate. Each procedure has its own evidence and approval requirements. Students should not assume that asking for help suspends a deadline or permits a student to sit an examination.

The attendance register is one part of the academic record. It is considered alongside course registration and the approved examination list. The department publishes the eligibility decision through the official student channel. A student who believes an eligibility decision contains a calculation or procedural error may use the relevant academic review route. The review should identify the course, dates, records, and remedy requested. Until a changed decision is issued, students should follow the official notice and contact the examination office promptly if the notice is unclear.

# University Rulebook

## 6.2 Grade Scale
Final course grades are reported as A, B, C, D, or F. A score of 85 or above earns A; 75 to 84 earns B; 65 to 74 earns C; 50 to 64 earns D; and below 50 earns F. Grade point average is calculated using credit-weighted grade values in the academic handbook. A mark is not rounded across a grade boundary unless the published assessment method expressly provides for rounding. Students may request a calculation review within ten working days of result publication. A review checks transcription, addition, application of weightings, and approved adjustments; it does not replace an examiner’s academic judgment.

## 6.5 Result Corrections
Where a verified clerical error changes the grade, the Registrar issues a corrected result and preserves the original record with an audit note. A correction request identifies the course, assessment, result, and alleged error. The office checks the authoritative mark sheet rather than relying on an informal message. A corrected transcript is released after the faculty confirms the change. The student is notified of both the decision and the right to appeal where the published process allows one.

## 11.2 Academic Appeals
A student may appeal a final grade or academic-progression decision within ten working days of publication. The appeal must identify a procedural error, new material evidence, or a calculation error. Dissatisfaction with an academic judgment alone is not a ground for appeal. The appeal is filed through Student Services with the decision, grounds, evidence, and requested remedy. A review panel should include members without a conflict of interest and considers the record created by the original decision-maker. The panel may uphold, vary, or return the decision for reconsideration under the published rules. The outcome and reasons are delivered through the portal.

Deadlines are calculated from official publication, not from when a student happens to read an unofficial copy. Students should retain the notice, submission receipt, evidence, and panel correspondence.

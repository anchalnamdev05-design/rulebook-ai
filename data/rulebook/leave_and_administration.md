# University Rulebook — Leave and Administration

## 14.1 — Academic Leave

A student who needs an approved period away from study submits an academic-leave request through the student portal before the proposed start date whenever circumstances allow. The request states the dates, programme, reason category, supporting evidence, contact details, and a plan for returning to study. The programme chair reviews academic feasibility and may seek advice from student services, the medical office, or the finance office. Approval is recorded with a start date, end date, conditions, and any courses or assessments affected. A student should continue attending and completing work until approval takes effect; submitting a request alone does not create leave.

Urgent medical circumstances may be notified by a representative while the student is unable to use the portal, but the formal record and evidence should be supplied as soon as reasonably possible. An approved leave period does not automatically remove a student from an examination list, hostel contract, scholarship condition, or fee account. Each affected office explains its separate procedure. Before returning, the student confirms the intended course load, contact details, and any required fitness or welfare meeting. A department may recommend a reduced or adjusted load where this is compatible with the programme and published assessment requirements.

## 14.2 — Short Absence and Communication

Short personal absence should be communicated to the instructor or programme office as early as possible, but communication does not automatically excuse attendance or assessment requirements. The instructor may share missed learning materials where reasonably available. Students are responsible for checking whether a quiz, laboratory, presentation, or submission occurred during the absence. A personal trip, family celebration, or transport delay is not described as an approved medical absence in the examination regulations. Where the rulebook is silent on a particular reason, the student should request a written decision from the responsible authority rather than infer an entitlement.

The university expects respectful and timely communication. Messages should identify the student, course, date, and specific request, and should not include unnecessary sensitive information. Confidential medical details should be directed to the medical or student-support office rather than sent to a public class channel. Staff record a decision and its scope when a request changes a deadline or attendance arrangement. A teaching assistant may forward a request but does not have authority to approve a policy exception unless the published procedure delegates that authority.

## 14.3 — Student Welfare and Accessibility

Student services offers confidential referral and coordination for wellbeing, disability access, counselling, financial difficulty, and safety concerns. A student may request an accessibility assessment and should provide relevant evidence through the secure channel. Approved adjustments are recorded in an accommodation notice that identifies the courses, dates, and practical measures. Adjustments may concern format, timing, room access, communication, or assistive technology; they do not guarantee a particular grade or remove an essential learning outcome. Students should share the notice with affected offices through the prescribed process and report a problem promptly.

A welfare referral is not a disciplinary finding and does not itself change an academic result. Staff should limit access to sensitive records and share information only for a legitimate support, safety, legal, or fair-process purpose. In an immediate safety emergency, the university may contact emergency services or a nominated contact according to the safety procedure. Students who need help outside office hours should use the current emergency contact listed in the portal. The handbook cannot determine an individual medical outcome; the designated professional or committee applies the relevant evidence and records the decision.

## 14.4 — Complaints and Service Review

A student who believes an administrative service was delayed, inaccessible, or handled incorrectly may submit a service complaint to the office manager within the period shown on the service page. The complaint identifies the request, dates, evidence, and remedy sought. A complaint about a grade, academic misconduct decision, residence sanction, or fee assessment must use the specialised review or appeal route instead. The service complaint process can investigate communication and procedure but cannot replace those decision routes or overturn a committee decision without authority.

The receiving office acknowledges a complete complaint and may ask for clarification. It reviews the record, seeks a response from the responsible team, and provides a reasoned outcome or explains why another process is required. A student may request a review of the service response where the published service standard allows it. Records should be factual, dated, and limited to the purpose of the complaint. Submitting multiple identical complaints does not accelerate a decision and may make it harder to identify the current request.

## 14.5 — Committees and Delegated Authority

University committees act within the terms of reference approved by the governing authority. The terms state membership, quorum, decision scope, conflict-of-interest rules, and reporting route. A committee may delegate routine administrative work where its terms permit, but a delegated officer cannot enlarge the authority or waive a requirement reserved to the committee. The minutes record attendance, the decision, material considerations, and any declared conflict. A student can request the written decision and applicable review route through the office named in the notice.

Different offices may advise on the same situation. The office that owns the relevant record and published procedure makes the formal decision, while another office may provide specialist advice. Students should not treat two informal answers as a change to the rule. If an apparent inconsistency remains, the student should cite both notices and request a clarification from the responsible authority. A correction should identify the replaced text and effective date so that students can determine which version applied to their submission, assessment, or application.

## 14.6 — Records Retention and Privacy

The university retains academic, finance, residence, conduct, and support records for the periods required by its records schedule and applicable law. Students may request access to personal records through the privacy contact, subject to identity verification and lawful exceptions. A correction request should identify the inaccurate field and provide reliable evidence. The university may preserve an audit history rather than delete a prior decision when the history is necessary to show how the record changed.

Official records are released only through approved channels. Students should use their institutional account and protect passwords, identity cards, and one-time verification codes. A student must not ask another person to impersonate them in a portal, examination, residence, or committee process. Suspected unauthorized access should be reported promptly to the information security contact. Privacy protections do not prevent necessary sharing for teaching administration, safety, fair process, lawful reporting, or a properly authorized review.

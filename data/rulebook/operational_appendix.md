# University Rulebook

## Administrative Interpretation and Evidence
University policies are applied to the facts, course, term, and status identified in the relevant provision. Staff should not expand a narrow exception because a similar situation feels fair, and students should not treat silence as permission. When a policy is silent, the responsible office identifies the nearest applicable procedure and states that the handbook does not decide the missing issue. A grounded response names the section, quotes the operative condition, and distinguishes a requirement from advice.

A complete submission contains the student identifier, course or account, relevant dates, a concise request, and every attachment listed by the form. Portal systems issue a receipt when validation succeeds. A saved draft, email sent to the wrong office, or conversation in a hallway is not an approval. If an attachment cannot be supplied, the student should explain why and request an extension before the deadline. Offices should record the date received, person deciding, evidence considered, decision, reasons, and notice method. These records protect students and staff when a result is reviewed.

## Deadlines and Notices
Working-day periods exclude published university closures. A deadline stated as ten working days starts on the date the official decision is published to the student portal, unless the decision states another start date. Students are responsible for keeping their university email, phone, and address current. An undeliverable message does not normally restart a published deadline when the account was active and correctly addressed. If a system outage prevents a filing, the student should keep a screenshot, submit as soon as access returns, and report the outage to the office.

## Evidence Standards
Evidence should be contemporaneous, legible, and connected to the person and date in question. Medical evidence should identify the treating service and relevant period without requiring unnecessary diagnosis details. Payment evidence should show the account or transaction reference. Attendance evidence should identify the class, date, and correction requested. Academic work should be submitted through the course channel so that the timestamp is preserved. Staff may request clarification when evidence is incomplete, but a request for clarification is not itself a rejection.

## Review and Appeals
A review checks whether the correct procedure was followed, whether arithmetic or transcription is accurate, and whether material evidence was considered. It does not automatically substitute a reviewer’s academic preference for an examiner’s judgment. An appeal filing identifies the decision challenged, the permitted ground, and the remedy requested. The panel may uphold the decision, correct a clerical error, return the matter for a new decision, or vary an outcome where the rule permits. The written outcome explains its reasoning and identifies any next route.

## Privacy and Access
Student records are accessed only by staff with a legitimate administrative need. A student may request their own official record after identity verification. Staff do not disclose medical or disciplinary details to classmates, residence groups, or unrelated instructors. A lawful hold or safety concern may limit release, but the office explains the limitation and the available review route. Data corrections require evidence because an inaccurate record can affect enrollment, fees, examinations, scholarships, and graduation.

## Communication and Escalation
Students should first use the office named in the provision. If no office is named, Student Services directs the request and records the referral. A complaint about delay is different from an appeal of a decision; the complaint asks for service review, while the appeal challenges the stated outcome on a permitted ground. Escalation should include earlier ticket numbers and receipts rather than restarting the matter without context. The University aims to provide a clear response, even when the answer is that the handbook does not cover the requested situation.

## Course and Assessment Practice
Course outlines state learning outcomes, assessment components, weighting, deadlines, permitted collaboration, and submission channels. Instructors may clarify an assessment method before work is due, but a clarification cannot retroactively change the published weighting without the approved curriculum process. Students should ask questions early when an instruction is ambiguous. A late submission policy concerns the timestamp and approved accommodation; it does not excuse plagiarism or unauthorized collaboration. Group work must identify each student’s contribution when the course requires it.

## Residence Safety
Residence staff maintain entry, leave, incident, and emergency records. A guest rule concerns access to a building and does not grant permission to use another person’s identity card or room. In an emergency, staff may contact the listed emergency contact and restrict access while safety is assessed. A resident should follow an evacuation instruction even if a leave request is pending. Conduct review considers notice, evidence, impact, and any prior written warning, and the student receives the applicable response route.

## Financial Administration
Fees are assessed by term and program. A posted balance may include tuition, residence, laboratory, examination, or replacement charges, each with its own description. Deferment is an approval status, not a general promise that a student can pay later. The Finance Office records whether a request was timely, complete, and approved. Registration holds are removed after the balance or approved arrangement is recorded. Students should not rely on a bank transfer until the university account displays the payment or Finance confirms the receipt.

## Graduation and Completion
A completion check compares credits, required courses, academic standing, outstanding fees, and approved substitutions with the program record. Students should request a degree audit before the final term. An audit is an administrative check and does not replace the published curriculum. A missing prerequisite or unverified transfer credit may delay award processing. The Registrar issues the official completion notice and explains any outstanding item. Students may correct a record or appeal a decision through the applicable route.

## Teaching, Registration, and Withdrawal
Registration confirms a student’s place in a course but does not replace the course outline or satisfy attendance. A student who changes a section should verify that the new instructor has the correct enrollment list. Withdrawal dates affect fees, transcript notation, progression, and examination eligibility, so the portal receipt should be retained. An advisor may explain the consequences of a choice, but the Registrar records the official change. A student should not assume that stopping attendance is the same as withdrawing. Where a course is cancelled, the department publishes an alternative, a refund process, or a record correction.

## Laboratories and Practical Work
Practical courses require safe participation, documented preparation, and completion of the practical outcomes. A student absent from a laboratory should use the course absence process and ask whether a supervised make-up is available. Make-up work is not automatic when equipment, safety supervision, or assessment timing prevents it. A medical accommodation can change how a practical is completed but does not silently remove the learning outcome. Instructors record attendance, submitted observations, safety training, and assessed performance separately. Students should report an equipment or access problem before the practical begins so it can be addressed fairly.

## Accessibility and Adjustments
An accessibility adjustment changes an approved method of participation or assessment without lowering the learning outcome. Examples may include additional time, an accessible room, assistive technology, or a changed communication format. The accessibility office issues an approval notice describing its duration and scope. Students should share the notice with the relevant office early enough for practical arrangements. An adjustment for one course does not automatically alter a different course’s assessment plan. Staff must protect the student’s privacy and should discuss implementation only with people who need the information.

## Examinations and Materials
The examination timetable, room, permitted materials, and candidate instructions are published by the Controller. Students should check the final version rather than rely on an earlier draft. Identity verification may be required at entry. Prohibited material includes any item identified in the candidate instructions, whether or not it is used. A student who believes an instruction is unclear should ask the invigilator before the examination starts. An invigilator may record an incident without deciding misconduct; the Integrity Officer and committee apply the disciplinary process. Examination scripts are retained under the records schedule and are not released through an informal request.

## Research and Original Work
Research students and course students must identify sources, data, collaborators, and permissions accurately. Fabricated data, altered records, copied language without attribution, and unauthorized assistance may be treated as misconduct. A supervisor may return a draft for correction before assessment, but a warning does not erase a later formal allegation. Students should keep dated drafts and approvals, especially where human participants, confidential data, or external material is involved. Research records are stored securely and shared only for approved academic or legal purposes. The responsible committee determines whether an ethics approval is required before data collection begins.

## Employment and External Commitments
The rulebook does not guarantee employment, immigration permission, professional licensure, or an outcome from an external organization. Students should consult the relevant external authority for those questions. A university scholarship, enrollment certificate, or transcript accurately describes the academic record but is not a promise of future employment. External placements may have additional attendance, safety, insurance, and confidentiality conditions. Students should disclose a conflict or placement restriction to the office managing the placement and obtain written guidance before accepting an incompatible commitment.

## Requests for Clarification
A clarification request should quote the provision, describe the concrete facts, identify the deadline, and ask a focused question. The office may respond with a direct answer, a form, a referral, or a statement that the rulebook is silent. A response that identifies silence is not a negative finding about the student; it means the cited materials do not establish an entitlement or prohibition. Students may ask which authority can decide a silent issue, but they should not convert a suggestion into an approval. The final decision must be issued by the authority named in the applicable procedure.

## Document Control
Each published handbook has a version, effective date, issuing authority, and correction process. A later version may alter a deadline or replace a section, so students should save the version cited by an official decision. A typographical correction should be identified as a correction rather than silently changing a substantive outcome. Offices maintain an index of active forms and archive withdrawn forms so that staff can identify the rule in force on a past date. When two documents appear inconsistent, the responsible authority should document which provision controls and why; students should receive that clarification in writing.

## Good-Faith Administration
Good-faith administration means applying the written rule consistently, considering evidence that the procedure permits, and giving notice of a material decision. It does not permit a staff member to waive an eligibility threshold without authority. It also does not prevent an authorized committee from considering an exception expressly described in the regulations. Students should describe facts accurately and promptly correct an error in a submission. Offices should not ask for irrelevant personal information, and they should explain a refusal sufficiently for the student to understand the next lawful route.

## Student Checklist
Before relying on a policy, identify the exact section, effective date, responsible office, required evidence, and deadline. Before submitting, check that the form names the correct course or account, dates are consistent, attachments open, and the portal shows a receipt. After submitting, save the receipt and monitor official messages. If the answer is delayed, use the ticket number and ask for a status update. If the answer is negative, read the reasons and permitted review ground before filing. If no provision answers the question, request an authority referral rather than guessing from a similar rule.

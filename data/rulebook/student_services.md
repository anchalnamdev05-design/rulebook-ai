# University Rulebook

## 10.3 Academic Misconduct
Academic misconduct includes plagiarism, unauthorized collaboration, impersonation, possession of prohibited material during an examination, and fabrication of academic records. An instructor must report suspected misconduct to the Academic Integrity Officer with supporting material. The officer conducts an initial review and gives the student a written summary of the allegation. The Student Discipline Committee may impose a warning, educational sanction, grade penalty, probation, or suspension after giving written notice and an opportunity to be heard. A decision includes reasons and information about the appeal process.

## 10.6 Disciplinary Hearing
A hearing is scheduled with reasonable notice and identifies the allegation, evidence, procedure, and possible outcomes. The student may respond to the allegation and identify relevant evidence under committee procedure. Interim measures may protect an examination or class without deciding the allegation. A committee member must disclose a conflict of interest and may be replaced. The written decision distinguishes established facts from submissions that were not accepted. An appeal must use the route and deadline stated in the decision.

## 13.4 Student Support
Students may ask Student Services for accessibility, wellbeing, enrollment, or records guidance. Staff explain the applicable form and deadline without promising an outcome that belongs to another authority. A support request is confidential except where disclosure is necessary for safety, legal compliance, or a fair decision. Students should identify any deadline that could be affected while a request is being processed. Staff record referrals so that the student does not have to repeat the complete history.

The University publishes forms and notices in the portal. A message from a friend, instructor, or residence group does not replace an official approval. Students should keep receipts, decision letters, evidence, and contact details for each case.
